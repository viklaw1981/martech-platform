A martech stack that gets
smarter with every lead

This document describes the architecture for a closed-loop acquisition platform — a system where every lead that converts into a policy teaches the system how to find better leads. Unlike traditional martech stacks that optimize for clicks and form fills, this one optimizes for issued policies and lifetime value.

The problem today

We're optimizing for the wrong thing

Most acquisition stacks optimize for cost-per-lead. Meta and Google learn to find people who click ads and fill out forms. But clicks and form fills don't pay premiums — policies do. The gap between lead volume and issued policies represents the core inefficiency this platform is designed to close.

What this platform does

Closes the loop on what actually matters

By feeding policy outcomes — bind rate, issued premium, LTV — back into every layer of the acquisition stack, the system continuously learns which channels, creatives, demographics, and behaviors actually predict a customer who buys and keeps a policy. Every lead makes the next lead cheaper and better.

Three design principles
01

Optimize for LTV, not leads

Every decision in the stack — bids, affiliate payouts, form length — is calibrated against issued premium and lifetime value, not cost-per-lead or click-through rate.

02

The system learns continuously

Policy outcomes loop back to the intelligence layer. The longer the platform runs, the more precisely it can identify which signals predict high-value customers.

03

Personalization at every step

From the first ad impression to the final form field, every interaction adapts to what's already known about the lead — reducing friction and improving conversion without compromising underwriting data quality.

Implementation phases
& Webflow integration

The platform is designed to be built in four phases, each delivering standalone value while laying the foundation for the next. Nothing is built for future phases that doesn't earn its keep in the current one.

1
Foundation — data, scoring, and traffic controls
Weeks 1–10

Build the core intelligence loop at low fidelity. A basic Cerebro model (even rule-based scoring is fine to start), the data layer for identity resolution, Apollo with static RPL tiers, and the bidding engine with manually configured bid modifiers. The feedback loop starts collecting outcome data immediately, so the model has training data ready for Phase 3.

Data layer + identity resolution Cerebro v1 — rule-based scoring Apollo + static RPL tiers Bidding engine + manual modifiers LMS integration + outcome tracking
What you can do after Phase 1: Route affiliate payouts based on lead quality signals. Start capturing the outcome data Cerebro will train on. Have a working closed loop, even if the intelligence is basic.
2
Funnel — dynamic landing page and form
Weeks 6–16 (parallel with Phase 1)

Build the two-step conversion funnel. The dynamic landing page with AI-generated content variants, bounce management, and retargeting pixels. The dynamic form with basic personalization from Cerebro scores and Apollo context. This is where you see conversion lift — and where the system starts generating the behavioral data the intelligence layer needs.

Dynamic landing page + AI content Bounce management + retargeting pixels Dynamic form + basic personalization NLP call center pipeline (Data Works)
What you can do after Phase 2: Run a fully personalized two-step funnel. A/B test page variants. Retarget non-converters. Form adapts to basic Cerebro score tiers.
3
Intelligence — Cerebro retraining and automation
Weeks 14–22

Cerebro graduates from rule-based scoring to a trained predictive model, using the outcome data collected in Phases 1 and 2. The multi-armed bandit replaces manual A/B testing. Apollo's RPL becomes continuously variable rather than tiered. The system starts compounding — each optimization cycle improves the next one.

Cerebro v2 — trained ML model Multi-armed bandit A/B automation Dynamic RPL in Apollo (continuous) Full personalization engine
What you can do after Phase 3: The system is fully self-optimizing. Bid modifiers, affiliate payouts, page variants, and form personalization all update automatically based on real outcome data.
4
Advanced loop — offline sync, compliance, and identity at scale
Weeks 20–28

Close the loop with the ad platforms directly. Offline conversion sync (Meta CAPI + Google Enhanced Conversions) teaches Meta and Google to optimize for issued policies, not just form fills. Compliance automation ensures TCPA consent, CCPA data handling, and model fairness are systematically enforced rather than manually checked. Identity resolution scales to handle full cross-channel deduplication.

Offline conversion sync — Meta CAPI Google Enhanced Conversions Value-based bidding (ROAS) Compliance automation layer Full identity graph at scale
What you can do after Phase 4: Ad platforms optimize for issued premium, not clicks. TCPA/CCPA compliance is systematic. The full closed loop is running — every policy issued improves every future acquisition decision across every channel.

On not making this a lifelong project

Phase 1 + Phase 2 together are the minimum viable platform — a working funnel with basic intelligence and a functioning feedback loop. That's 16 weeks of focused build. Phases 3 and 4 are compounding improvements, not prerequisites. The temptation to over-engineer Phase 1 (perfect identity resolution, fully trained ML model, complete compliance framework) is the most common reason these projects stall. Ship a working loop first. The data it collects makes everything in Phases 3 and 4 significantly better.

Webflow integration

Webflow works well for some parts of this stack and hits hard limits on others. The key is knowing which layer owns what.

Works well in Webflow

Landing page shell + brand

  • Page layout, typography, and brand design
  • CMS collections for content variant management
  • Conditional visibility via Webflow interactions (reads URL params / cookies set by Cerebro upstream)
  • Pixel and script injection via custom code head/body
  • Bounce management scripts (injected as custom code)
  • A/B testing via injected third-party tool (Convert.com, VWO)
Webflow's hard limits

Dynamic form (step 2)

  • Webflow forms are static — no conditional branching
  • No field pre-population from external APIs at render time
  • No programmatic field order changes based on Cerebro score
  • No server-side logic for form assembly
Recommended approach: Use Webflow for the landing page shell and brand layer. Move the form (step 2) to a custom-coded component — either an embedded React/Vue app hosted on a subdomain, or a configurable form builder like Typeform or a custom implementation. Cerebro signals travel via URL parameters and first-party cookies set at the traffic acquisition layer. Webflow reads these to personalize the landing page; the form component reads them to personalize the survey. The intelligence layer (Cerebro, Apollo, bidding engine) lives entirely outside Webflow and communicates through APIs — Webflow is the front-end renderer, not the logic layer.
